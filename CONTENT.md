## Problem

**One program. Many people. More AI tools every month.** Enterprise programs lose alignment when decisions live in meeting notes, consultants keep separate files, some teams have deeply adopted AI while others have not, and every approved AI product works from a different version of the truth. The answer is not to force everyone onto one AI. The answer is to give every approved surface — human and machine — access to the same governed program playbook.

## Operating model

**Keep the systems. Add alignment.** Your SAP and enterprise platforms remain the systems of record. A client-owned program repository sits between those systems and the people or AI tools producing program work. Every consultant and every approved AI surface retrieves from the same current context.

- **Existing systems of record** — SAP, Microsoft 365, Teams, SharePoint, Jira, Azure DevOps, ServiceNow, Cloud ALM, GitHub — the authoritative environment remains in place and in control.
- **Client-owned program playbook** — Decisions, definitions, requirements, evidence, terminology, training, approved AI instructions, and provenance — structured for reuse and human review.
- **People and AI delivery surfaces** — Consultants, program employees, and approved AI tools receive only the context and permissions their task requires — and return their work through the same record.

## SAP semantic layer & sovereign AI (reference model)

**The SAP semantic layer and sovereign models work in both directions.** Aligned with CISA guidance on securing the data used to train and operate AI systems. The SAP semantic spine — ACDOCA, master data, process definitions, and reporting semantics — can be leveraged by client-owned sovereign models. In the other direction, sovereign models can produce additional structured data in a form and repository that Joule and other approved SAP surfaces can access efficiently. The client keeps custody of the reasoning at both ends.

### Direction one · SAP → sovereign

**Sovereign models leverage the SAP semantic layer.** Client-owned models retrieve from governed SAP semantics rather than a scraped or re-implemented shadow copy. ACDOCA remains the ledger spine, and the meaning of the enterprise flows into the model with its provenance intact.

- Governed retrieval, not exfiltration
- Meaning travels with the data
- ACDOCA as the ledger spine

### Direction two · sovereign → Joule

**Sovereign models feed structured data Joule can use.** The output of sovereign reasoning — decisions, evidence, structured extracts, prepared context — lands in a repository shaped so that Joule and other approved SAP surfaces can access it efficiently, without re-training the model or bypassing SAP controls.

- Structured form, not free text
- Repository Joule can reach
- No proprietary Joule product

## SAP delivery — six work packages

**Accelerate SAP Activate. Keep the knowledge.**

1. **Activate Mobilization** — Configure the program operating structure around SAP Activate phases, workstreams, roles, governance, decision paths, gates, and deliverable controls. *Outputs: delivery plan, RACI, governance calendar, RAID structure, decision register, deliverables index, repository baseline.*
2. **Fit-to-Standard Acceleration** — Prepare workshop packs and source material. Capture discussions, assumptions, actions, and decisions. Classify gaps and preserve traceability. *Outputs: workshop packs, process decisions, fit-gap register, requirements, action log, evidence links.*
3. **Template and Rollout** — Convert approved processes into reusable rollout assets. Organize local requirements and exceptions. Preserve deployment learning between business units and sites. *Outputs: global template, localization register, rollout playbook, site-readiness packs, reusable checklists.*
4. **Change and Adoption** — Convert program decisions and process knowledge into role-based communications, learning paths, procedures, and support materials. *Outputs: stakeholder maps, impact assessments, communications, learning paths, SOPs and job aids, knowledge base.*
5. **Go-Live Stabilization** — Coordinate hypercare, command-center information, triage, ownership, escalation, and communications. *Outputs: command-center view, issue register, daily briefing, support procedures, ownership map, KT pack.*
6. **Continuous Improvement** — Organize and assess the post-go-live backlog. Clarify business need, dependency, ownership, evidence, and release priority. *Outputs: prioritized backlog, dependency map, business cases, release roadmap, benefits tracking.*

## PMO assistants trained on the program's own repositories

**Lillian's repositories become the training substrate for PMO assistants that find savings during discovery and planning.** This is additive, not defensive. The project artefacts and context reasoning Lillian's programs already produce — decisions, definitions, process rules, ACDOCA-side semantics, workshop packs, fit-gap registers, prior forecasts, prior renewal spend — are the same material that trains new PMO assistant models. Once those assistants are in the room during discovery and planning, they read the client's existing estate against the incoming scope and surface savings early: renewals to consolidate, licensed but under-used surfaces to activate, duplicated tooling to retire, scope items already covered by capabilities the client owns.

Why discovery and planning: by hypercare, most program spend is committed. By fit-to-standard, most tool choices are anchored. Discovery and planning is the one window where a well-briefed PMO assistant — trained on the client's own repositories and running against the current inventory — can move numbers on the business case.

Standards:

- Trained on the client's own repositories
- Reads the existing estate against incoming scope
- Surfaces savings while they are still recoverable
- Feeds forecasts and business cases directly (integration, refinement, and adoption cost included)
- Compounds across programs — the repository is the strategic asset

## Inventory before purchase

**Strategy and forecasting see the existing toolkit before a third-party licence is proposed.** Most enterprise clients already own more AI and data capability than any incoming program realises — Microsoft 365, Teams, Microsoft 365 Copilot, SharePoint, Power Platform, Purview, Azure AI Foundry, Fabric, existing SAP capabilities such as Joule, Build, and Datasphere, and previously licensed agent frameworks. Strategy and forecasting have to inventory that estate first. Third-party purchases are proposed only when the gap is documented, the existing capability was tested, and the incremental value is defensible against renewal budgets.

Standards:

- Documented existing-tool inventory
- Fit test against existing surfaces first
- Documented gap and defensible increment
- Forecasting reflects the existing estate
- Named-owner accountability for the inventory (refreshed on the six-week cadence)

## Trade-secret structured-data refinement

**We don't hand people Claude Pro accounts. We refine the trade-secret material they're allowed to use.** A licensed AI surface is only as good as the structured material it's allowed to reason over. Trade-secret structured-data refinement is the discipline of taking the client's confidential meaning — decisions, definitions, process rules, ACDOCA-side semantics, approved evidence — and shaping it into structured records that every approved surface (Claude Pro, Perplexity Pro, ChatGPT, Microsoft 365 Copilot, Azure AI Foundry agents, MCP-connected agents) can consume inside the controls the client's legal and security teams have already defined. Adoption is then measured against how well those refined records are actually being used, not against license counts.

Standards:

- Refined records, not raw dumps
- Approved-tool consumption protocol
- Baseline before adoption claims
- Fitness check on a fixed cadence (six weeks)
- Named-owner accountability (business, security, review)
- Signal stays client-held
