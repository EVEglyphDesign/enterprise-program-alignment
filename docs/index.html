How the acceleration actually works

Prove the connections. Then bring in the team.

Most program delay is access delay. The team is assembled, and then it waits weeks for credentials, network paths and approvals nobody mapped in advance. We reverse the order: the data paths are opened, tested and validated before a single delivery consultant is mobilized.

01

Map the access protocols on arrival

We document every source the program will need—the data lake, the document repositories, SAP and the surrounding systems—and the access method each one already uses. Service accounts, network routes, approval owners, classification and retention rules. This is a survey of what exists, not a request to change it.

02

Work to the client's current standards

Access is requested the way the client already grants it. Where a standard exists, we use it. We do not introduce a parallel method, a new platform or an exception process for the convenience of the program.

03

One coordinated request, not a trickle

All access is raised together as a single approved plan with named owners and a stated purpose per source. Security, network and platform teams review one package of known scope instead of months of one-off exceptions arriving after the program has already started.

04

Connect in one compressed window

Once approved, the connections are established all in, inside a short window, rather than opened gradually as each workstream discovers it is blocked. This is where the schedule is recovered.

05

Point to point, tested and validated

Each connection is proven end to end against real data before it is relied on: read path, permissions, refresh behaviour, volume and failure handling. Results are recorded in a connection register the client owns and keeps.

06

Then assemble the team

Delivery consultants arrive to working access and current data. Discovery starts with evidence in front of it instead of a credentials queue, and the first weeks produce findings rather than tickets.

Trade-secret structured-data refinement

Structure the record so it can be trusted—and defended.

Refinement is the discipline that turns raw project material into structured, cited, review-ready records. It supports the reasonable-measures test for trade-secret protection and makes the repository fit for AI to consult.

01

Refined records, not raw dumps

Every submission is structured, dated, named and cited to the source—so the repository stays a working asset rather than a shelf.

02

Classification on the way in

Material is tagged for sensitivity, ownership and retention at capture—so retrieval and access controls have something to enforce.

03

Provenance travels with the artefact

Source, author, decision reference and approval status stay attached—so every downstream use can be traced back.

04

Reasonable measures, documented

Access limits, agreements, awareness and handling rules are recorded—so the client can show what was done, not only intended.

05

Superseded, not deleted

Prior versions are marked and kept alongside the current record—so history remains reviewable and corrections stay auditable.

06

Fit for AI to consult

Structured well enough for retrieval to return the right passage, and cited well enough for the answer to be defensible.

Inventory before purchase

Plan against what is already licensed. Then decide.

Strategy, planning and forecasting have to be well informed of the tools already available inside the client's existing infrastructure—before rushing to buy third-party alternatives.

01

Start from the estate

List what the client already owns: SAP modules, Microsoft 365, collaboration platforms, security stack, existing AI surfaces and current agent frameworks.

02

Match scope to capability

Test each incoming scope item against the estate before writing a purchase line—new spend only where the gap is real.

03

Fair-test first

When an in-house surface plausibly covers the need, give it a documented, time-boxed trial before comparing to third-party pricing.

04

Include the true cost of "free"

Integration, refinement, adoption and support cost sit alongside licence cost in the business case—both sides count.

05

Decision on the record

Buy, build or activate—the choice is recorded with the evidence behind it, so the next program starts from a shorter list, not a blank page.

Contribution & meeting standard

Every meeting produces a repository entry. Every entry improves the next meeting.

Meeting standard

  1. Named owner and named decision-makers before the meeting starts.
  2. Agenda drawn from the repository—so context is shared, not reconstructed.
  3. Decisions, exceptions and open items captured in-session, not after.
  4. Actions have owners and due dates before people leave the call.
  5. Recorded Teams sessions and their transcripts are filed to the project repository and stay available to participants for the life of the program, with consent and retention agreed in advance.
  6. The record is filed to the repository within one working day.

Contribution standard

  1. Every contribution is attributable—named author, date, source.
  2. New material is cited to its evidence, not to a memory of it.
  3. Superseded material stays alongside the current version, marked.
  4. Anything shared with an AI surface is drawn from the same record.
  5. The repository is the record. Chat transcripts and drafts are not.

Human control points

The session is the foundation. The review is the control.

Two practices carry most of the weight. Recorded working sessions become foundation material in the client's own repository—available to participants throughout the program, not archived at the end—so anyone can reassemble how a design direction was reached instead of relying on recollection. And every artefact is reviewed under version control, so approval is a recorded event against a specific version, not an email that agreed to something nobody can now produce.

01

Recorded sessions become foundation material

Teams recordings and transcripts from workshops, design sessions and decision meetings are filed to the project repository with consent and retention agreed up front. Discovery, fit-to-standard and design discussions stop evaporating when the call ends.

02

Transcripts stay available during the program

Participants can go back through the meeting transcripts while the work is live—reassembling the history of a topic, the reasoning behind a design direction and who raised which constraint. It is a working reference for the team, not a closeout archive nobody opens.

03

Transcripts are refined, not dumped

A session is filed as a dated, attributed entry with the decisions, exceptions, open items and owners drawn out and linked back to the recording. That is what makes a topic searchable months later. Raw audio alone is not a record; it is an unindexed liability.

04

Every artefact is versioned

Designs, specifications, requirements, test evidence and training material live as versioned files in the client's repository rather than as attachments circulating in mailboxes and chat threads.

05

Review is a recorded event

A named reviewer approves a specific version. The comments, the changes requested and the sign-off are kept alongside the artefact, so the control point can be shown months later without reconstructing it.

06

AI output enters as a proposal

Assisted work arrives as a proposed change against the current version, with its sources cited. It becomes program commitment only when a person accepts it. The accountable human is named on the record.

07

The trail outlives the team

What changed, who changed it, when, against which decision and out of which session stays readable after consultants rotate off. Access to recordings and transcripts follows the client's own consent and retention rules. The next team inherits the reasoning, not just the deliverable.
