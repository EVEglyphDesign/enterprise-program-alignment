# PMO assistants trained on the program's own repositories

This is the additive value case for the repository. It is not defensive framing — it is the reason the repository pays for itself early in every engagement.

## The premise

Lillian's programs already produce a rich body of project artefacts and context reasoning: decisions, definitions, process rules, ACDOCA-side semantics, workshop packs, fit-gap registers, RAID history, prior forecasts, prior renewal spend, adoption evidence, handover material. That body of work is the substrate. New PMO assistant models are trained and grounded on it, and are then deployed into the phases where they can still move numbers on the business case: discovery and planning.

## Why discovery and planning

By hypercare, most program spend is committed. By fit-to-standard, most tool choices are already anchored. Discovery and planning is the one window where a well-briefed PMO assistant — grounded in the client's own repositories and running against the current inventory — can:

- Consolidate renewals the client is about to sign twice.
- Activate licensed but under-used surfaces (M365 Copilot, Power Platform, Purview, Azure AI Foundry, Fabric, Joule, Build, Datasphere, prior agent frameworks).
- Retire duplicated tooling the incoming program was about to sit on top of.
- Defer third-party proposals until an existing surface has been fairly tested.
- Show scope items that are already covered by capability the client owns.

Each of those is a savings finding that arrives before the licence is signed and before the workstream is anchored.

## What the assistants do in each phase

### Discovery

- Read the client's existing capability inventory against the incoming scope.
- Cross-reference incoming scope items with prior program artefacts (has this been decided before? Where? By whom?).
- Surface renewal calendar overlaps and consolidation candidates.
- Flag under-used licensed surfaces that could take on incoming scope.
- Produce a discovery savings register, each item cited to the artefact behind it.

### Planning

- Feed the discovery savings register into the planning forecast.
- Include integration, refinement, and adoption cost alongside the licence line so the business case is defensible against renewal budgets.
- Draft the fair fit-test plan for each proposed third-party purchase (see [`INVENTORY-BEFORE-PURCHASE.md`](./INVENTORY-BEFORE-PURCHASE.md)).
- Draft the consumption protocol for each surface that will carry program-sensitive material (see [`TRADE-SECRET-REFINEMENT.md`](./TRADE-SECRET-REFINEMENT.md)).
- Baseline the four adoption dimensions from prior-program evidence so outcome-uplift language has a client-held comparison behind it.

## Why this compounds

Every discovery the assistants run adds to the repository. The next program starts from a stronger baseline. The savings surfaced in planning become the recurring dividend of maintaining the repository — not a one-off finding tied to a single engagement.

This is what makes Lillian's repositories a strategic asset rather than a documentation cost. The work of capturing decisions, definitions, and reasoning is the work of training the next PMO assistant that will pay for itself in discovery.

## Grounding, not replacement

The assistants are grounded on the client's material and cite it. Named consultants remain accountable for every finding that becomes a program commitment. The assistant accelerates discovery and planning; it does not replace the review, approval, and decision authority that lives with people.
